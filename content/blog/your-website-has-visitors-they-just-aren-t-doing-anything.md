Picture a shop. People walk in, stare at the shelves for a few seconds, and walk out again without saying a word. Nobody buys, nobody asks a question, nobody even takes a sweet from the bowl.

That is a website with a low conversion rate. The traffic is there. The results are not.

## First, what is a conversion rate?

It is the share of visitors who do the thing you want them to do. Ask for a quote. Call you. Book a slot. Buy. Divide the number of people who did it by the number of people who visited, and you have your rate.

A made-up example: 200 people visit your site this month and 4 request a quote. That is 2%. Not good or bad on its own, but now you have a number to beat.

## What actually helps

No magic, no growth hacks. Mostly it is about making things easy.

1. **One goal per page.** If your page asks the visitor to call, mail, follow, subscribe and read the blog, they will do none of those. Pick one main button.
2. **A headline that says what you do.** "Welcome to our website" is a shop sign that says "Shop". Say what you do, for whom, in five seconds.
3. **Speed on a phone.** Most visitors arrive on one. If your page takes forever to load, they never see how good it is.
4. **A small next step.** A form with twelve fields feels like a tax return. Ask for the name, an email and one question. You can ask the rest later.
5. **Proof that you exist.** Real photos, real examples, real reviews, a name and a face. People buy from people they can picture.
6. **Contact details you cannot miss.** If someone wants to reach you, do not make them hunt for it.
7. **No surprises.** If you can, show a price or a range. Visitors who guess the worst usually leave.

## What does not help

- A pop-up that appears before anyone has read a single word.
- A carousel that slides away just as you start to read it.
- A menu with fourteen items, each as important as the others.
- Stock photos of people shaking hands in a very clean office.

## The five-second test

Show your homepage to a friend for five seconds, then close the laptop. Ask three questions: What does this company do? Who is it for? What should I do next? If your friend hesitates on any of them, you just found your first improvement.

## And then: measure it

You cannot improve what you never count. Make sure your site tracks the one action that matters, like a quote request, and not just the number of visits. Check it once a month. Test your own form on a phone while you are at it. You would be surprised how often it quietly stopped working.
