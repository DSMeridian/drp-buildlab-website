There comes a moment in every entrepreneur's life when someone says: you should do something with video. A silence usually follows, and an image of yourself dancing in your workshop.

We believe short video is here to stay. Not because an algorithm wants it, but because people want to see who they are going to buy from. That is an opinion, and one we apply ourselves. Just without the choreography.

> A customer buys from a person. A video shows that the person exists.

## Why it works

- **People want to see who you are.** A face and a voice do in thirty seconds what text can't do in three pages.
- **It is low-threshold.** Watching thirty seconds costs nobody any effort.
- **You can reuse it.** The same video fits on your site, in a newsletter and on social media.

## What you can film

- **A day in your workshop,** in thirty seconds.
- **Before and after.** A bare garden and a garden with life. An old website and a new one.
- **A customer's question,** answered in one minute.
- **A mistake you almost made,** and what you learned from it.
- **How something is made.** People enjoy watching craftsmanship.

## A scenario in four sentences

Suppose you have a garden company. A video of thirty seconds can go like this: a corner of a bare garden (the hook), what the customer wanted (the problem), two days of work in ten seconds (the work), and the finished garden with one sentence from the customer (the result).

Four pieces, one story. You don't need more.

## What to skip

**Don't**

- Copy a trend that has nothing to do with your trade.
- Open with a ten-second intro with music.
- Let AI invent and edit everything.

**Do**

- Film something only you can: your work.
- Start with the result or the question.
- Use an honest image with a short sentence of your own.

## How to fit it into your week

1. **Film in batches.** One hour, five videos.
2. **Use a fixed template.** Hook, problem, work, result.
3. **Pick a fixed moment each week** to film or edit.
4. **Keep everything in one place,** with a clear file name.
5. **Let someone else edit** if that saves you time.

## What to measure

Views are nice, enquiries are better. Look at how long people keep watching, who responds and above all how many people get in touch afterwards. A video with few views and three quote requests is a better video than one with thousands of views and none.

## Rights and consent

- **People on camera.** Ask permission, especially from customers and employees.
- **Music.** Only use music you have the rights to, for example from the platform's library.
- **Brands and other people's work.** Leave out third-party logos and photos, or ask.
- **Children.** Don't film them without explicit permission from their parents.

## Practical, in five steps

1. **Hold your phone upright.** Vertical is the norm.
2. **Find light by a window.** You need nothing more.
3. **One idea per video,** no longer than a minute.
4. **Add subtitles.** Many people watch without sound.
5. **Put it on your website too,** with the right service or project.

The worst thing you can do is wait until your video is perfect. The second worst is dancing.
