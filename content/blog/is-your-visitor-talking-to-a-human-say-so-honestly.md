A visitor types: 'Hi Sarah, can I still order today?' Sarah answers within a second, friendly and flawless. Too flawless. Sarah turns out to be a script. And the visitor doesn't feel helped, but deceived.

Chatbots have become much better in recent years. That makes the temptation big to give them a human name, a profile photo and a friendly little sigh. Bad idea.

> A bot that pretends to be human wins a conversation and loses a customer.

## What the rules say

In essence, the European AI Act requires that people know they are talking to an AI, unless that is obvious. The exact dates and exceptions are still being adjusted and your situation may differ, so check them with your legal adviser or the authorities. One thing doesn't change: being honest costs nothing.

## Why honesty is also good sales

Honesty is not only an obligation. Visitors who know they are talking to a bot expect less of it and are therefore less easily disappointed. Visitors who discover they have been misled tell others.

A bot that knows its limits also comes across as more reliable than a bot that has an answer for everything. 'I don't know that, I'll connect you with a colleague' is sometimes the best sentence it can say.

## Don't, do

**Don't**

- 'Hi, I'm Sarah!'
- 'I think you'll definitely get twenty percent off.'
- 'I'll always help you, until the end of time.'

**Do**

- 'Hi, I'm this company's AI assistant. How can I help?'
- 'For prices and discounts, a colleague will gladly help you further.'
- 'If I can't work it out, I'll say so and bring in a human.'

## What your bot had better not answer itself

- **Prices and discounts,** unless they are fixed and up to date.
- **Legal, medical or financial advice.**
- **Complaints and conflicts.** A human belongs there.
- **Data about other customers,** in any form.
- **Promises about delivery times** it can't check.

## Your checklist for an honest chatbot

1. **Give it an honest name.** 'Assistant' or 'Concierge', not Sarah.
2. **Say it in the first sentence.** That it is an AI, and what it can do.
3. **Always leave a way to a human open.** A button, an email address, a phone number.
4. **Be clear about privacy.** What do you do with the conversations? And let the chat load only after consent for cookies.
5. **Limit what it talks about.** Prices, legal and medical advice: refer onwards.

## How to test it before you let it loose

1. **Make a list of twenty real customer questions** and look at the answers.
2. **Try to mislead it.** Ask questions it must not answer, and see if it politely declines.
3. **Check the tone.** Does it sound like you, or like a leaflet?
4. **Read every conversation in the first weeks,** with respect for privacy.
5. **Adjust and repeat.** A bot is never finished.

## Questions for your supplier

- Where is the data stored, and in which country?
- Are conversations used to train models?
- Who helps with a wrong answer, and how fast?
- Can I view, export and delete conversations?
- Does the contract say who is liable for a mistake?
- Can I set myself what the bot may not talk about?

## Who is responsible if the bot gets it wrong?

You are. To your customer, you are the sender, even if someone else's system writes the sentences. So agree in advance who gives support for a wrong answer and how fast. A bot may be funny. It may not lie about who it is.
